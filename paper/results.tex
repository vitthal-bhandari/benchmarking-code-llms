\section{Results}

We evaluate three memory policies on 99 SWE-Bench Verified instances. A1 is plain
ReAct with no memory. A2 compresses on a harness threshold. A3 is ACM's untrained
Base agent, which is given the context management tools and decides when to use
them. Table~\ref{tab:memory-arms} compares three models at a 64K budget.
Table~\ref{tab:cap-sweep} and Figure~\ref{fig:cap-sweep} sweep Qwen3.5-9B across
32K, 64K and 128K. Within any row group the model, sampling configuration and
budget are fixed, so the only variable is the memory policy.

\paragraph{The value of context management depends on the budget, and changes sign.}
At 32K the baseline resolves 2 of 99 while memory resolves 27 to 36. At 64K the
gap is 19 against 36 to 38. At 128K the baseline overtakes both, 48 against 39 to
43. The benefit is therefore not a property of the method but of the regime it
runs in. ACM report a single operating point at 128K, where their own measured
gap is $+0.019$, which is the end of the curve where the mechanism has least to do.

\paragraph{Our baseline reproduces theirs.}
At 128K we measure ReAct at 0.485 against their reported 0.489 on the same model
and benchmark, with an independent harness. At 128K we see a small harm from
memory where they report a small benefit. Both effects are small and ours rests
on 99 instances, so we do not read this as a contradiction.

\paragraph{Memory improves coverage rather than per-patch quality.}
Almost all of the gain comes from instances the baseline loses outright. At 64K,
splitting by whether A1 survived, A1 resolves 19 of the 34 instances it completes
and none of the 65 it loses to context. On those same 65, A2 resolves 19 and A3
resolves 21. On the 34 the baseline already handled, all three arms sit within six
points of each other. A3 converts its hard-instance submissions at 57\%, which
matches the baseline's 58\% on easy instances, so the patches are not worse. The
lower aggregate conversion of the memory arms is a pooling effect from attempting
harder problems.

\paragraph{No model manages its own context.}
Across every run, 2 compressions out of more than 1000 were voluntary, both on
Qwen3.5-9B at 128K and both firing well below the forced threshold. Every other
compression was triggered by the harness or forced at 95\%. Models do comply when
told: 906 nudges produced 817 compressions at 32K. An earlier configuration that
asked for \texttt{manage\_context} as a bash string rather than a registered tool
was ignored entirely, and would have reported that compliance as zero.

\paragraph{Forcing by prompt is unreliable under pressure.}
At 32K, A3 still loses 26 instances to out-of-context while A2 loses 1, on
comparable edit counts of 817 and 815. A2 compresses unconditionally, whereas A3
asks and roughly a tenth of the time the answer is late or absent. This is the
clearest argument in our data for post-training the behaviour rather than
prompting for it.

\paragraph{Two limits of the current setup.}
First, a turn limit of 500 still truncates the memory arms, and no run that hits
it ever resolves. At 32K, 68 instances across A2 and A3 end this way, so the
benefit we measure at tight budgets is a lower bound. Inspecting those
trajectories, 67\% of their final 50 commands are new at 32K against 25\% at
128K, so the tight-budget runs are cut off while still working whereas the 128K
runs are looping. Second, agents do not convert a larger budget into new work:
the share of commands that exactly repeat an earlier command rises from 23\% at
32K to 55\% at 128K. That is why 128K does not help.
